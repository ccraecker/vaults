After considering the costs of buying, it is important to understand the home loan financing framework in Singapore which includes CPF usage rules and borrowing limits, as these factors can affect your loan options and overall financial situation.

Here is a summary of the rules on maximum limits for new housing loans:

## Loan Tenure

Loan tenure refers to the duration or the time period a borrower has to repay a loan, including both the principal amount and interest. It essentially defines the length of time you will be making payments to the lender.

It directly impacts both the monthly repayment amount and the total cost of the loan due to interest accumulation. Choosing a longer tenure means lower monthly payments but higher overall interest paid, while shorter tenures result in higher monthly payments but lower total interest.


## Loan-To-Value (LTV) Limits

LTV refers to the loan amount as a percentage of the property’s value. For example, if an individual borrows $150,000 to purchase a property valued at $1,000,000, the LTV is 15%.![[Pasted image 20260821161852.png]]


## Mortgage Servicing Ratio (MSR)

MSR refers to the portion of a borrower’s gross monthly income that goes towards repaying all property loans, including the loan being applied for.

## Total Debt Serving Ratio (TDSR)

TDSR refers to the portion of a borrower’s gross monthly income that goes towards repaying the monthly debt obligations, including the loan being applied for.

TDSR limits ensure that borrowers are not over-leveraged for property purchases. It also complements the existing loan-to-value (LTV) limits on property purchases.
