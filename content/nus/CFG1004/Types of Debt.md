Debts are typically categorised as either **secured or unsecured**, or by repayment structure such as **revolving or non-revolving**, with each type carrying distinct features and risks.

## Secured vs. Unsecured Debt

**Secured debt** is backed by collateral (e.g. mortgage, car loan). If you default, the lender can claim the asset. Because this lowers their risk, secured loans usually come with lower interest rates.

**Unsecured debt** has no collateral (e.g. credit cards, personal loans). It usually carries higher interest rates due to greater risk for the lender.

## Revolving vs. Non-revolving Debt

**Revolving debt** allows you to borrow, repay, and borrow again up to a credit limit (e.g. credit cards, lines of credit).

**Non-revolving debt** is a fixed loan that is repaid over time in instalments and cannot be re-borrowed once repaid (e.g. personal loans, mortgages, student loans).


**Good debt** generally refers to borrowing that can help you build wealth over time. Examples include home loans (like mortgages), student loans, and small business loans. These types of debt are often seen as **investments in your future**, as they have the potential to increase your earning potential or grow in value.

**Bad debt**, on the other hand, typically refers to high-interest, unsecured borrowing such as credit card and personal loans. This type of debt is often used to purchase items that quickly lose value and **do not generate any financial return**. The most common example is credit card debt, especially when it is used for everyday expenses or to buy depreciating assets.


## Which debt to pay off first?

Knowing which debts to prioritise for repayment is critical to maintaining financial health. Generally, it is advisable to focus first on paying down high interest, unsecured debt, such as credit card balances. At the same time, it is important to maintain timely payments on all debts to avoid penalties and protect your credit score.
