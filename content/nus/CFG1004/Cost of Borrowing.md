**  
When you borrow, you repay more than you borrow due to interest charges.**

**The rate you pay depends on your creditworthiness (as reflected in your credit report), the type and duration of the loan, and whether you provide collateral.**

**Ultimately, taking a loan means using someone else’s capital, typically from lenders or banks.**


## **Cost of borrowing in comparison**

Different types of debt carry varying costs of borrowing. Secured debt, like mortgages, generally has lower interest rates than unsecured debt, like credit cards, due to the reduced risk for the lender. Non-revolving loans typically have lower interest rates than revolving credit lines, but revolving credit offers more flexibility. The cost of borrowing also depends on factors like the lender, the borrower's creditworthiness, and prevailing interest rates.

## **Using credit and loans wisely is part of building long-term financial readiness.**

Borrowing money is not inherently good or bad, it depends on your circumstances and what the loan is for.

**A helpful rule of thumb: credit works best when it is an investment in your future**, like buying a home, starting a business, or investing in further education. In short, borrow when it builds your future, not just your lifestyle.

**Thinking about getting a credit card?** Many offer perks like miles or cashback, and if you pay off your full balance by the due date, you can enjoy up to eight weeks of “free” credit. Only spend what you already have in your account and do not borrow against future earnings.

## How does this eight weeks of "free" credit work?

The billing cycle typically lasts about 30 days, after which a statement is issued summarising your transactions. A due date is then set, typically 25 days from the statement date, by which time payment must be made to avoid late fees and interest charges.

The interest-free period refers to the time between the statement date and the due date, during which you can pay off new purchases without incurring interest.

**To maximise this benefit, consider planning larger purchases at the start of your billing cycle**, this gives you the longest possible time to repay the amount before interest applies
