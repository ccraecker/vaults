## **What is the Credit Score?**

The credit score is a numerical representation of an individual's creditworthiness, typically calculated by the [Credit Bureau Singapore (CBS)(opens in a new tab)](https://www.creditbureau.com.sg/about-cbs.html).

Your credit score determines how capable and responsible you are when it comes to repaying your debt. It is your credit history which lenders or financial institutions consider before approving applications for loans and credit lines.

Sometimes, even potential employers look at your credit score before they hire you. This is particularly common for roles in the banking and finance industry, and may also be used by employers to assess a candidate's financial stability.

## **How is credit score calculated?**

The credit score is calculated using past credit information to predict future repayment performance.

It ranges from 1000 to 2000, and it is tagged to risk grades ranging from AA to HH. AA represents the lowest probability of ***default** (i.e. 0.27% or less) and HH represents the highest probability of the consumer defaulting (i.e. 3.48% or more)


**In finance, a "default" occurs when a borrower fails to meet the legal obligations of a loan, typically by not making the required payments on time. For example, if you do not pay your credit card bill minimum payment or loan instalment by the due date, your account may be marked as in default. This can negatively impact your credit rating**

## **Who is the Credit Bureau Singapore (CBS)**


CBS is licensed and regulated by the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) under the Credit Bureau Act 2016.

CBS is not involved in the credit lending decision process of the bank or financial institution, but it **plays a key role in tracking your credit history** and it is what the bank and financial institutions refer to when deciding whether to trust you with loans.

**Why credit score matters?**

A good credit repayment history makes it easier for you to qualify for loans and credit cards in the future. It might also be used by your employer to assess your financial stability, particularly for roles in the banking and finance industry.

**Tips to improve credit score**

1. **Strive to pay your bills in full and on time, all the time**: Consistently paying your credit card bills, loans, and other obligations by their due dates is one of the most important factors in building a strong credit history. Keeping your credit utilisation low (generally below 30% of your available credit) is beneficial.
    
2. 2
    
    **Pay down your debts as soon as possible:** Debt is like quicksand, it can be easy to get into but hard to get out of. The longer you carry debt, the more interest you pay. Aim to reduce your principal quickly to avoid compounding costs and free up resources for other financial goals.
    
3. 3
    
    **Limit the number of unused credit accounts:** Inactive credit cards can incur fees and affect your credit if balances go unpaid. Cancel unused accounts to simplify credit management and avoid unnecessary charges.
