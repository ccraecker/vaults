**What is BNPL?**

BNPL is a short-term installment loan that splits a purchase into fixed payments, often interest-free if you pay on time.

For example: You buy a $400 jacket. You pay $100 at checkout followed by three monthly payments of $100 to the BNPL provider.

BNPL has become an increasingly common feature of online shopping. In July 2021, it was estimated that 19% of Singaporeans had used a BNPL product. (Milieu Insight, “The Big Picture: Payments, Singapore Report” <[https://www.mili.eu/insights/big-picture-report-payments-singapore(opens in a new tab)](https://www.mili.eu/insights/big-picture-report-payments-singapore)> (July 2021)

**Why do people use BNPL?**

- 1
    
    **Split payments:** Allows consumers to break up a purchase into smaller, manageable payments over time.
    
- 2
    
    **Cash flow support**: Helps deal with irregular income or unexpected expenses by smoothing out payments.
    
- 3
    
    **Promotions & discounts**: Often paired with special deals, making it appealing particularly for younger, tech-savvy users.
    
- 4
    
    **Interest-free (when used wisely):** If paid on time and in full, BNPL can offer interest-free credit, potentially cheaper than using a credit card.
    
- 5
    
    **Simple approvals:** Offers a financing alternative for underbanked individuals who may not qualify for traditional credit cards or loans.
    

**Risks and Downsides**

- •
    
    **Overspending:** Tiny instalments mask real cost, leading to impulse buys.
    
- •
    
    **Limited affordability checks**: Users, especially young or vulnerable individuals, may take on more debt than they can manage.
    
- •
    
    **Risk of unnecessary debt:** Easy access can lead to borrowing for non-essential purchases.
    
- •
    
    **High fees for missed payments:** Late or missed payments often come with significant penalties.
    
- •
    
    **Optimism bias:** Consumers tend to overestimate their ability to repay, underestimating the risks.
    
- •
    
    **Encourages impulse spending:** The ease of “one-click” credit can fuel overspending and poor financial habits.


## **When might BNPL be a good idea**


You need to buy something important (e.g. work laptop or school supplies) and know exactly how you will repay each instalment.

You are good at managing your money and always pay your bills on time. You intentionally use BNPL to spread payments over time as part of a planned budget and not as a fallback.

You are a little short on cash right now and just need some extra time to pay things off without affecting your other financial commitments


In November 2022, the Singapore FinTech Association announced the [BNPL Code of Conduct.(opens in a new tab)](https://singaporefintech.org/bnpl-code-of-conduct/) Among other things, this code of conduct specifies how platforms have the responsibility of protecting consumers from getting into financial difficulties, which is why the maximum amount of outstanding payments for any individual is limited to $2,000.


## **Better alternatives to BNPL**

Plan ahead by tracking your cash flow, building an emergency fund, and setting a budget for purchases instead of relying on BNPL as a safety net.

Consider using credit cards especially if you plan your purchases at the start of the billing cycle to take advantage of interest-free credit, as covered in the previous topic.


In summary

Buy Now, Pay Later (BNPL) can be a useful short-term financial tool if used responsibly. It is best used for specific, manageable purchases, not as a way to afford a lifestyle.
